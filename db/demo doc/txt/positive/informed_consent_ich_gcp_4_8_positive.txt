1.	Why is this study being done?

This study is being done to learn whether CLN-201 is safe and effective for adults with moderate inflammatory disease. CLN-201 is an investigational medicine, which means it is still being studied and has not been approved for this use.

2.	Why have I been asked to participate?

You may be eligible to participate because you are an adult with moderate inflammatory disease and may meet the study requirements.

3.	Do I have to take part?

 
No. Taking part in this study is completely voluntary. You may choose not to participate or you may leave the study at any time without penalty or loss of benefits to which you are otherwise entitled.

4.	What will happen if I take part?

If you agree to participate, you will be asked to sign this form before any study-specific procedures are performed.
You will go through screening tests to determine whether you are eligible. If you qualify, you will be assigned by chance (like flipping a coin) to receive either CLN-201 or placebo. A placebo looks like the study drug but does not contain active medicine.
Neither you nor the study doctor will know which treatment you are receiving during the study, unless unblinding is necessary for medical reasons.
Study procedures may include:
  Review of your medical history and current medications   Physical examinations and vital signs
  Blood and urine tests
  Questions about your symptoms and health
  Completion of questionnaires about quality of life   Taking study medication once daily for 12 weeks
  Clinic visits during screening, baseline, Weeks 2, 4, 8, and 12, and a follow-up visit about 4 weeks after treatment ends

5.	What are my responsibilities?

  Attend scheduled study visits
  Take the study medication as instructed
  Tell the study doctor about any side effects or new symptoms   Tell the study team about any new medicines you take
  Follow study instructions, including pregnancy precautions if applicable

6.	What are the risks and discomforts?

Because CLN-201 is investigational, there may be risks and side effects that are known and others that are unknown.
Known or possible side effects may include:
  Headache   Nausea
 
  Fatigue   Rash
Dizziness
There may also be risks related to blood draws, such as pain, bruising, bleeding, lightheadedness, or rarely infection.
If you are pregnant, planning pregnancy, or breastfeeding, you should not take part in this study. If applicable, the study doctor will discuss contraception requirements with you.

7.	Are there any benefits?

You may or may not receive direct medical benefit from participating. Information learned from this study may help future patients with inflammatory disease.

8.	What are my alternatives?

You do not have to take part in this research study to receive care. Alternatives may include standard medical care, other approved treatments, supportive care, or no participation in this study. The study doctor can discuss these options with you.

9.	Will I be paid?

You will not be paid for taking part in this study unless required by local site arrangements. You may be reimbursed for reasonable study-related travel or parking costs if allowed by the study site.

10.	What if I am injured because of the study?

If you believe you have been injured as a result of taking part in this study, contact the study doctor immediately. The study team will explain available medical treatment and local policies regarding compensation or treatment for research-related injury.

11.	Will my information be kept confidential?

Your personal and medical information will be kept as confidential as allowed by law. Your records may be reviewed by the sponsor, ethics committees, regulatory authorities, and authorized representatives for purposes related to the study, but your identity will be protected to the extent possible.
